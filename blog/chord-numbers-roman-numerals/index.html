Chord numbers explained: play any song in any key

When musicians say “it’s a one-four-five” they’re using a secret code that works in every key. Learn it once and you’ll be able to transpose songs, find their key and talk with any band.

7 min read · The Chordseed Journal · Leer en español

Every key has a family of seven chords

Take a major scale, build a chord on each note using only notes from that scale, and you get seven chords. They always follow the same pattern of major and minor:

NumberIiiiiiIVVvivii°
Typemajorminorminormajormajorminordim

Capital Roman numerals mean major chords, lowercase means minor, and the little circle means diminished. The pattern never changes, only the letter names do.

The chord families of the most common keys

The vii° chord is left out because it’s rarely used in pop and rock.

What each number does

  • I is home. Songs usually start and end here.
  • IV is a step away from home: open and relaxed.
  • V is tension. It wants to go back to I.
  • vi is the emotional, minor side of home.
  • ii and iii are softer connectors, often leading to V or IV.

Transposing becomes easy

The famous I–V–vi–IV progression is the same idea in every key. Only the letters change:

CGAmFin C
GDEmCin G
DABmGin D

So if a song is too low for your voice, write it down in numbers, choose a higher key and read the new letters from the table. A capo does the same on guitar: with a capo on the 2nd fret, G shapes sound in the key of A.

The Nashville number system

Session players in Nashville use the same idea with regular numbers: 1 – 5 – 6m – 4. The band can follow a chart in any key the singer asks for, on the spot. Roman numerals or Nashville numbers, it’s the same code.

How to find the key of a song

  1. Listen for home. The chord that feels like rest, usually the first or last one, is probably I.
  2. Check the family. Look for the row in the table that contains all (or almost all) the chords of the song.
  3. Follow the 7 chord. A chord like D7 almost always points to its I chord, G.
  4. Major or minor? If home is a minor chord (say Am), the song is in A minor, which shares its family with C major.
