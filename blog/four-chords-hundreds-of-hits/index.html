The 4 chords behind hundreds of hits

Four chords, in one order, have carried an enormous number of hit songs. Here is what they are, why they work, and how to use them without sounding like everyone else.

6 min read · The Chordseed Journal

The progression

In Roman numerals it's I – V – vi – IV: the home chord, the tension chord, the sad chord and the open chord. In the key of C:

CGAmFkey of C

And in other easy keys:

GDEmCkey of G
DABmGkey of D
EBC#mAkey of E

You'll hear it in songs like "Let It Be" by The Beatles (verse, C–G–Am–F) and "With or Without You" by U2 (D–A–Bm–G). Once you know it, you start hearing it everywhere.

Why it works

  • It has a bit of everything. Rest (I), tension (V), emotion (vi) and openness (IV), in just four bars.
  • Every chord shares notes with the next. C and Am share two notes, Am and F share two notes. The changes feel smooth, never jumpy.
  • It loops perfectly. The F wants to go back to C, so you can repeat it forever under a verse and chorus.

Same chords, different starting point

Start the loop on a different chord and the mood changes completely:

OrderIn CFeels
I – V – vi – IVC G Am FUplifting, anthemic
vi – IV – I – VAm F C GEmotional, a bit dark
IV – I – V – viF C G AmHopeful, floating
I – vi – IV – VC Am F GNostalgic, 50s ballad

Five ways to make it yours

1. Change the rhythm of the changes

Don't give each chord one bar. Try two bars of C, one of G, one of Am, and F only for the last two beats. Same chords, new song.

2. Swap one chord

Replace F with Fm on the last pass, or G with Em. One change is enough to make it feel personal.

3. Add color notes

Cmaj7 – G – Am7 – Fmaj7 sounds softer and more modern. Csus2 and Fadd9 make it shimmer on guitar.

4. Move the bass

Play G with B in the bass (G/B). Now the bass goes C – B – A – F, a smooth walk down.

5. Use it for one part only

Keep the four chords for the chorus and write a verse with something else, like Am – Em – F – C. The contrast makes the chorus land harder.

Is it okay to use it?

Yes. Chord progressions aren't owned by anyone. What makes a song yours is the melody, the lyrics, the rhythm and the sound, and the four chords are just the soil they grow in.
